Curtains do more than give you privacy. They control both the light and the heat that come into a room.
That's why using the same fabric throughout the house almost always works badly for at least one room.

## West-facing: the problem is afternoon heat

Afternoon sun comes in low and hot. Rooms on this side need blackout fabric with a heat-reflective lining,
and the track should extend past the window frame on both sides, because far more heat sneaks in around the edges of the curtain than most people expect.

If it's a bedroom, adding window film will cut the load on your air conditioner much more noticeably than simply choosing a thicker fabric.

## East-facing: morning light is easier to manage

Morning sun is cooler, so these rooms work well with a sheer layer paired with a blackout layer.
During the day, open the blackout and let the sheer filter the light. The room stays bright enough without switching on the lights.

## North-facing: don't go heavier than you need

North light is steady and doesn't bring much heat. Heavy blackout fabric here just gives you a room that's dark all day with no thermal benefit.
Sheers or adjustable wooden blinds are a better fit.

## South-facing: you need something adjustable

The angle of the sun changes most across the year on this side. Adjustable systems such as venetian blinds or light-filtering roller blinds
are more flexible than blackout fabric alone.

## What to check when measuring on site

- Only measure after the ceiling and painting work is finished
- Check whether the wall the track will be fixed to is brick or a lightweight partition, since they need different anchors
- Leave clearance for the air-conditioner unit and any cornice
