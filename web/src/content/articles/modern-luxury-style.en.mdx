Modern Luxury isn't about heavy ornament or expensive decorative pieces.
It comes from good-looking materials, crisp joints and lighting that has been placed with intent.
It works in a detached house or a condo alike, as long as you understand that luxury comes from the details, not the size of the room.

## Colour palette: go dark, but give the eye somewhere to rest

Modern Luxury tends to use deep neutrals such as charcoal, chocolate brown, deep green or navy,
accented with brushed gold, brass or bronze in small doses, on handles and table legs for example.

In a small condo, going dark on every surface feels oppressive. Try this instead:

- Use the dark colour on **a single feature wall**, such as the bedhead wall or the wall behind the TV
- Choose a **wallpaper** with a marble or silk pattern and a subtle sheen, so the wall picks up the light
- Keep floors and ceilings lighter than the walls so the room doesn't feel squashed

## The floor is the foundation of luxury

The floor is the largest surface in any room, so it changes the feel of the space more than anything else.
**Herringbone SPC flooring** is a classic look that suits Modern Luxury.
Install it Glue Down for a tight, flat pattern, or if you prefer something calmer, long dark wood-effect planks also look upmarket.

In a detached house where the ground floor picks up moisture from the soil, moisture-resistant materials keep this look intact, without swelling or warping in the rainy season.

## Curtains and light: what makes a room look expensive

- **Full-height pleated curtains from ceiling to floor** make the ceiling feel higher and the room look custom-made
- **Fabrics with weight and a slight sheen**, such as light velvet or satin-finish fabric, in the living room
- **Double-layer curtains**, a sheer on the inside and blackout on the outside, so you can adjust the light through the day

West-facing rooms with large windows should also have **window film**,
because dark fabrics that take direct afternoon sun every day fade quickly, and the room builds up heat.

## Built-ins and glass: the most visible details

Modern luxury is about continuous lines. Built-ins designed to meet the ceiling and sit flush with the walls
look far more polished than a collection of freestanding pieces. Worth considering:

- **Wardrobes with bronze or grey glass doors** and interior lighting
- **A built-in TV wall** that hides every cable and device
- **A glass shower screen**, slim-framed or frameless, for a hotel-style bathroom
- **A glass partition with a black or gold frame** to separate the living room from a study while keeping them visually connected

## Keep the luxury going through the whole house

Plenty of houses and townhouses have beautiful interiors, but extensions like a rear kitchen or carport roof look like a different house.
If you're planning an extension, match the material tones to the interior, and make sure the connection to the existing structure is done properly.

> Luxury that lasts is luxury that's easy to look after. Choose materials that handle sun and humidity first, then choose the pattern.
