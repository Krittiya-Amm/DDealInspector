Most people who inspect their own unit only look at what's obvious: is the paint smooth, are the tiles neat?
But the problems that are hardest and most expensive to fix after transfer are usually the ones you only find with the right tools or a trained eye.

## What to prepare before you start

- Your sale contract and the unit floor plan from the developer
- A torch, a tape measure, and a marble or ping-pong ball to test floor levels
- A fully charged phone to photograph anything that looks wrong
- At least two hours. Don't squeeze the visit in between other appointments

## 12 things to check

1. **Floor level.** Put the marble down and watch where it rolls. A noticeably sloping floor means furniture will never sit straight.
2. **Bathroom floor fall.** Pour water and make sure it all drains to the outlet without pooling.
3. **Leaks around window frames.** This is where the water stains on your walls a year from now usually start.
4. **Wall cracks.** Look at the direction of a crack, not just its length.
5. **Doors and windows.** Open and close each one fully. Every lock should work and nothing should scrape.
6. **Sockets and earthing.** Test every outlet, not just a few at random.
7. **Consumer unit and breakers.** Check the labels actually match the circuits.
8. **Water pressure.** Run several taps at once and see if the flow holds up.
9. **Waste pipes under the sinks.** Look for seepage and sewer smells coming back up.
10. **Ceilings.** Look for water stains and uneven board joints.
11. **Developer-supplied built-ins.** Open every door and drawer, and check they're firmly fixed to the wall.
12. **Balcony.** Check the drainage fall and that the drain isn't blocked.

## What to do when you find a problem

Take photos and note the exact location, then put everything in writing and submit it to the juristic person or the sales office.
Don't rely on a verbal conversation alone. When you follow up later, you'll have no record of what you actually reported.

> Inspection day before transfer is when you have the most negotiating power.
> Once you've signed for the unit, everything becomes a warranty claim that waits in a queue.
