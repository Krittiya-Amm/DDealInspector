Minimalism doesn't mean an empty room. It means every object in the room has a reason to be there.
In the 25 to 35 square metre condos most city dwellers in Thailand live in, minimalism is what lets a small room breathe. It's a practical choice, not just a look.

## The heart of minimalism is storage you can't see

Almost every minimalist room that looks good in photos has more hidden storage than visible furniture.
Without planning this first, everyday clutter will be back on your countertops within weeks.

- **Floor-to-ceiling built-in wardrobes** make full use of vertical space and leave no gap on top for dust
- **Handleless flat-panel doors** with push-to-open or finger-groove pulls turn a whole wall into one clean plane
- **Beds or sofas with drawers underneath** for spare bedding and seasonal items
- **Kitchen racks that tuck inside cabinets**, such as an in-cabinet dish rack, keep the counter clear

## Colours and materials: fewer, but durable

For a Thai home, go for warm white, soft grey or beige rather than stark white.
The sun here is intense, so bright white walls throw glare around the room and show fingerprints and dust far more easily.

For floors, think about humidity before looks.
**SPC flooring** suits a minimalist room well: a light wood pattern makes the space feel continuous, and it handles moisture better than laminate.
If the existing floor is already level, you can choose either Click Lock or Glue Down, depending on site conditions.

If a wall needs some depth without adding clutter, a subtle textured wallpaper, such as a linen or plaster effect, keeps the room from feeling flat without any extra decor.

## Daylight and heat have to be handled together

Minimalism loves bare windows and lots of light, but a west-facing condo gets very hot in the afternoon.
Ways to deal with it without breaking the clean look:

- **Roller blinds or pleated curtains in the same tone as the walls**, so they disappear when open
- **Window film** cuts heat without keeping the curtains closed all day, so the room still gets light
- **Curtain tracks recessed into the ceiling.** If the ceiling isn't done yet, raise this before work starts

## Small room, separate zones

In studio condos, the bed and the desk often end up in the same line of sight.
A **glass partition** or an open-style room divider separates the zones while letting light through, so the room doesn't feel chopped up.
In the bathroom, swapping a shower curtain for a clear glass shower screen makes a small space feel bigger straight away.

## Common mistakes

- Buying freestanding furniture piece by piece until the room is full, before storage is planned
- Choosing white for everything, so the room feels cold and unfinished
- Forgetting walking space around the bed and in front of wardrobes, so doors can't open fully
- Not planning power points early, and ending up with extension leads across the floor

> A minimalist home that actually works starts with "where will this live?" before "how good will the room look?"
