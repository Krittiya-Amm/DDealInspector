When defect reports to a developer stall, it's rarely because they refuse to fix things. More often, the information they receive isn't clear enough for their contractors to act on.

## Why reports don't get results

- Writing "there's a mark on the wall" without saying which room or which wall
- Sending close-up photos so tight you can't tell where in the room they were taken
- Reporting only by phone or in person on site, with nothing in writing
- Lumping every issue into one item, so nothing can be tracked point by point

## A report structure that actually works

Every item should cover four things.

1. **Location.** Bedroom 1, east wall, about 1.2 metres above the floor.
2. **What you found.** Describe what you see. Don't diagnose the cause on the contractor's behalf.
3. **Photos.** A wide shot for context and a close-up for detail.
4. **Priority.** Must fix before transfer / should fix / monitor.

## Why referencing standards helps

When a report cites measurable criteria, such as the fall of a bathroom floor or an actual moisture reading,
the conversation shifts from "it feels uneven" to "it measures this much, which is outside tolerance."
That's language the developer's construction team can act on straight away.

## How to follow up so things don't go quiet

Ask for the name of the person handling your case and a repair date in writing, then book a re-inspection after that date.
Don't sign for the transfer until a re-inspection confirms that everything has really been fixed.
